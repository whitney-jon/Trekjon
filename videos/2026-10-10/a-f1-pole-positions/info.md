# Who has the most pole positions in F1 history?

## Title
Who has the most pole positions in F1 history?

## Description
The race starts at the end of the 1991 Formula 1 season, with Ayrton Senna on 60 career pole positions, almost double Jim Clark's 33. Who has taken the most pole positions in F1 history?

This race tracks career Formula 1 Grand Prix pole positions at the end of every season from 1991 to 2025. The record passes from Ayrton Senna to Michael Schumacher and then to Lewis Hamilton, while Sebastian Vettel and Max Verstappen climb into the top five.

For fans watching from Europe: eight of the final top ten are European. Hamilton, Jim Clark and Nigel Mansell race under the British flag, Schumacher, Vettel and Nico Rosberg under Germany's, Alain Prost under France's and Verstappen under the Netherlands'. Senna (Brazil) and Juan Manuel Fangio (Argentina) are the only two from outside Europe.

Key Highlights:
Senna's Mark: Ayrton Senna finished his career in 1994 on 65 poles, a record that stood until 2006.
Schumacher Takes Over: Michael Schumacher's 66th pole, at the 2006 San Marino Grand Prix, broke Senna's record, and he finished on 68.
Hamilton's Century: Lewis Hamilton took his record 69th pole at Monza in 2017 and finished 2025 on 104.
The Chasers: Max Verstappen reached 48 poles by the end of 2025, behind only Hamilton, Schumacher, Senna and Vettel (57).

Data includes: Lewis Hamilton, Michael Schumacher, Ayrton Senna, Sebastian Vettel, Max Verstappen, Jim Clark, Alain Prost, Nigel Mansell, Nico Rosberg, Juan Manuel Fangio, and other historical top-ten contenders.

Data Source: Wikipedia, List of Formula One polesitters (1991–2025); RacingNews365 all-time pole positions.
