# Who has won the most premier-class races in MotoGP history?

## Title
Who has won the most premier-class races in MotoGP history?

## Description
The race starts at the end of the 1991 season, with Giacomo Agostini out in front on 68 premier-class wins and Mike Hailwood a distant second on 37. Who has won the most premier-class races in MotoGP history?

This race tracks career premier-class Grand Prix wins (500cc and MotoGP, sprint races not counted) at the end of every season from 1991 to 2025. Mick Doohan's 1990s domination, Valentino Rossi's record run and Marc Márquez's climb shaped the leaderboard.

For fans watching from Europe: seven of the final top ten are European. Italy has Rossi, Agostini and Francesco Bagnaia, Spain has Márquez, Jorge Lorenzo and Dani Pedrosa, and Britain's Mike Hailwood is still seventh more than 50 years after his last premier-class win.

Key Highlights:
The Doohan Era: Mick Doohan won 12 races in 1997 and finished his career on 54, second on the all-time list behind Agostini.
Rossi Takes the Record: Valentino Rossi's 69th win, at Indianapolis on 14 September 2008, broke Agostini's record of 68.
Márquez Moves Up: Marc Márquez's 69th win, at the Sachsenring on 13 July 2025, took him past Agostini into second.
The Final Mark: Rossi ends the race on 89 wins, 16 clear of Márquez on 73.

Data includes: Valentino Rossi, Marc Márquez, Giacomo Agostini, Mick Doohan, Jorge Lorenzo, Casey Stoner, Mike Hailwood, Eddie Lawson, Dani Pedrosa, Francesco Bagnaia, and other historical top-ten contenders.

Data Source: Wikipedia list of 500cc/MotoGP race winners and season results pages (1991–2025); Crash.net all-time MotoGP winners list.

## Event cards
- 2008: Valentino Rossi breaks Agostini's record of 68 wins (Crash.net, 15 Sep 2008, "Rossi's 69th makes Indy MotoGP history": 69th premier-class win at the rain-shortened Indianapolis GP, 14 Sep 2008). CSV: Rossi 62 at end of 2007, 71 at end of 2008.
- 2025: Marc Márquez passes Agostini into 2nd place (motogp.com German GP report, 13 Jul 2025: "It's 69 wins now", surpassing Agostini). CSV: Márquez 59 at end of 2024, 73 at end of 2025; Agostini 68.

For 2023–2025 only main races are counted; sprint winners were checked separately and excluded. Every rider's seasons sum to his published career total.
- Career totals checked against two lists: Wikipedia "List of 500cc/MotoGP race winners" (to the 2026 Aragon GP) and Crash.net "All-time MotoGP winners list" (4 Oct 2026). Both include 2026 wins for active riders (Márquez 77/79), so end-of-2025 values were derived from the season counts: Márquez 73 (bikesales.com.au, 15 Sep 2025: Misano was his "73rd premier-class win", his last win of 2025).
- Pre-1990 totals (Agostini 68, Hailwood 37, Duke 22, Surtees 22, K. Roberts 22, Spencer 20, Sheene 19, Mamola 13, Read 11; Lawson 30, Gardner 15, Schwantz 8, Rainey 4 at end of 1989) from both lists and the Wikipedia rider pages for Lawson, Rainey, Schwantz and Gardner.
- Intermediate spot-checks: FIM, 14 Feb 2024 (Rossi 89, Agostini 68, Márquez 59, Doohan 54, Lorenzo 47; matches the CSV at end of 2023); motogp.com, Aug 2024 (Bagnaia equals Schwantz on 25); Crash.net 2008 (Rossi's 69th); motogp.com 2025 (Márquez's 69th).
- 2026 is excluded (season in progress).
- Flags: nations as listed by the FIM/MotoGP results (Italy, Spain, Australia, USA, Great Britain). Ties (Lawson/Pedrosa/Bagnaia on 31; Duke/Surtees/Roberts on 22) are ordered by who reached the total first.
- Note: one Wikipedia season page (2015) says Rossi won 5 races in its text, but its race table shows 4 (Qatar, Argentina, Assen, Silverstone); 4 is used and gives his published total of 89.
