## Description
Michael Schumacher was the first Formula 1 driver to make $30 million a year, and by 2005 he had banked more than $300 million in salary at Ferrari. For 13 years nobody came close. Which F1 driver has earned the most?

This race tracks F1 career salary earnings season by season from 2005 to 2026, with each bar in the colours and logo of the driver's team. Schumacher leads until Fernando Alonso passes him in 2018 after his big-money McLaren years. Then Lewis Hamilton, after a record $76 million season at Mercedes in 2020, takes No. 1 in 2021 and pulls away, finishing on $725 million, ahead of Alonso ($546m) and Schumacher ($386m). Max Verstappen ends 2026 fourth on $380m, just $6m behind Schumacher.

For UK fans: Lewis Hamilton is F1's richest driver by salary, and Jenson Button ($159m) and Lando Norris ($117m) both make the all-time top 10.

Key Highlights:
Schumacher's Reign: Michael Schumacher was F1's top career earner until 2017.
Alonso's Moment: Fernando Alonso was No. 1 from 2018 to 2020.
Hamilton's Record: Hamilton's $76 million in 2020 is the biggest single-season salary in the data, and he has led since 2021.
Max Is Coming: Verstappen's $70m-a-year Red Bull deal puts him $6m behind Schumacher.

Data includes: Lewis Hamilton, Fernando Alonso, Michael Schumacher, Max Verstappen, Sebastian Vettel, Kimi Räikkönen, Charles Leclerc, Jenson Button, Lando Norris, Nico Rosberg, and other historical top-ten contenders.

Data Source: Spotrac F1 driver salary histories (cash totals by season, 1996–2026)

## How it works
- Values are F1 salaries (cash including bonuses, as listed by Spotrac), added up season by season. Endorsements and personal sponsors are not included.
- Spotrac's F1 histories start in 2005 for every driver except Schumacher, whose 1996–2004 Ferrari salaries ($274m) are carried into his 2005 total. Earlier pay for other drivers (e.g. Räikkönen 2001–04, Button 2000–05, Barrichello, Coulthard) is not in Spotrac, so it is not counted.
- 2026 is included at Spotrac's 2026 contract values (the season is nearly over).
- Three seasons missing from Spotrac were filled from the same contract: Hamilton 2014 $27.5m (his 2013 figure), Button 2011 $12m (his 2010 figure), Alonso 2006 $7m (his 2005 figure).
- Bars follow the driver's team each season; retired drivers keep their last team. Verstappen's 2015 season is shown as Toro Rosso.
- Bar colours follow each team's livery of the time: McLaren chrome silver to 2014, graphite 2015–16, papaya from 2017; Mercedes silver, with the black cars of 2020–21 and 2023–25; Renault blue/yellow 2005–06, yellow 2007–08, orange 2009; Lotus Renault GP and Lotus F1 black and gold; Williams navy to 2008, white 2009–20 (Martini 2014–18), navy from 2021; Honda white 2006 then the 'earth' car 2007–08; Brawn white and fluoro. Retired drivers keep the colours of their last season.
- Toyota and Force India bars use the teams' white liveries so their logos stay visible.
