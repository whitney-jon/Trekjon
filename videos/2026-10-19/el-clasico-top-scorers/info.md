# Who has scored the most goals in El Clásico history?

## Description
Real Madrid and Barcelona have met more than 260 times in official competition since 1902, and the next El Clásico is this Sunday. Who has scored the most goals in El Clásico history?

This race tracks every goal in official Clásicos (La Liga, Copa del Rey, Supercopa, Copa de la Liga, European Cup/Champions League) from 1950 to May 2026, counting goals from the earliest matches from the start. Barcelona's César holds the record until Alfredo Di Stéfano passes him in 1962, and the Real Madrid great keeps it for 52 years, through Puskás, Gento, Santillana and Raúl. Then Lionel Messi's 2014 hat-trick takes him clear, and he finishes on 26, eight ahead of Di Stéfano and Cristiano Ronaldo.

For European fans: the top ten includes Argentina's Messi and Di Stéfano, Portugal's Ronaldo, France's Benzema, Hungary's Puskás and Uruguay's Luis Suárez, alongside Spanish legends Raúl, Gento, César and Santillana. Real Madrid players are shown in white, Barcelona players in garnet.

Key Highlights:
Di Stéfano's Record: Alfredo Di Stéfano passed César in 1962 and held the record for 52 years.
Puskás's Year: Ferenc Puskás scored two Clásico hat-tricks in 1963.
Messi's Hat-Trick: Messi's hat-trick at the Bernabéu in March 2014 took him from 18 to 21 and past Di Stéfano.
Still Playing: Vinícius Júnior (8), Raphinha (7), Kylian Mbappé (6) and Robert Lewandowski (6) are the leading active scorers.

Data includes: Lionel Messi, Alfredo Di Stéfano, Cristiano Ronaldo, Karim Benzema, Raúl, César Rodríguez, Paco Gento, Ferenc Puskás, Santillana, Luis Suárez, and other historical top-ten contenders.

Data Source: Wikipedia, List of El Clásico matches (every official match and scorer, 1902–10 May 2026, and the all-time top scorers table)

## How it works
- Counts all official Clásico goals, own goals excluded. Friendlies and other unofficial matches are not counted.
- Starts in 1950 with all earlier goals already counted (Josep Samitier leads on 10, scored for both clubs: 7 for Barcelona, 3 for Madrid; shown in Barcelona colours). Cup matches are dated by the year they were played; pre-2020 Supercopas at the start of the season, January Supercopas since 2022.
- Bar colours: Real Madrid white with gold edge, Barcelona garnet with blue edge.
- Flags: birth nation for Di Stéfano (Argentina) and Puskás (Hungary), who both later played for Spain; Eulogio Martínez Paraguay; Luis Suárez = the Uruguayan (2014–20); Luis Suárez Miramontes (Spain, 1950s–60s) is a separate row.
