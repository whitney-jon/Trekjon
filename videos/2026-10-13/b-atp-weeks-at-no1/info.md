# Who has spent the most weeks as world No. 1 in men's tennis?

## Title
Who has spent the most weeks as world No. 1 in men's tennis?

## Description
The race starts at the end of 1991, with Ivan Lendl holding the record on 270 weeks as ATP world No. 1 and Jimmy Connors just behind on 268. Who has spent the most weeks as world No. 1 in men's tennis?

This race tracks each player's total weeks at No. 1 in the ATP singles rankings at the end of every year from 1991 to 2025. The record passes from Lendl to Pete Sampras, then Roger Federer and finally Novak Djokovic, with Rafael Nadal climbing past the 1980s greats along the way.

For fans watching from Europe: four of the final top ten are European: Serbia's Novak Djokovic (428), Switzerland's Roger Federer (310), Spain's Rafael Nadal (209) and Sweden's Björn Borg (109). Sweden's Stefan Edberg is 11th on 72, and the new generation, Italy's Jannik Sinner and Spain's Carlos Alcaraz, are climbing just behind.

Key Highlights:
Sampras Takes the Record: Pete Sampras began his 271st week at No. 1 on 2 August 1999, breaking Ivan Lendl's record of 270.
Federer's Reign: Roger Federer spent 237 straight weeks at No. 1 from February 2004 and passed Sampras's 286 in July 2012.
The Djokovic Era: Novak Djokovic began his 311th week at No. 1 on 8 March 2021, breaking Federer's record of 310.
The Final Mark: Djokovic's last week at No. 1 was in June 2024, leaving the record at 428 weeks.

Data includes: Novak Djokovic, Roger Federer, Pete Sampras, Ivan Lendl, Jimmy Connors, Rafael Nadal, John McEnroe, Björn Borg, Andre Agassi, Lleyton Hewitt, and other historical top-ten contenders.

Data Source: ATP Rankings history via Wikipedia, List of ATP number 1 ranked singles tennis players (1973–2025); TooManyRackets ATP No. 1 history; Guinness World Records.
