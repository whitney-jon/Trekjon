# Which club has the highest Premier League net spend?

## Description
When the Premier League began in 1992, Blackburn Rovers were the big spenders, and a €7m net outlay was enough to top the table. More than three decades and billions of euros later, which club has the highest net transfer spend in Premier League history?

This race tracks every club's cumulative net transfer spend (money spent on transfers minus money received) in its Premier League seasons, from 1992/93 to the 2026 summer window. Blackburn, Liverpool, Newcastle and Leeds take turns at the top in the 1990s, then Chelsea go clear after Roman Abramovich's 2003 takeover. Manchester City take over in 2015/16, Chelsea's record €562m net spend in 2022/23 puts them back in front, Manchester United lead in 2025/26, and City's 2026 summer finishes the race with them back on top.

For European fans: figures are in euros, from Transfermarkt. City (€2.16bn), United (€2.11bn) and Chelsea (€1.84bn) are the only clubs past €1.5bn, with Arsenal, Spurs and Liverpool all close to €1.4bn.

Key Highlights:
The Abramovich Effect: Chelsea's net spend went from about €100m to €434m in their first two seasons under Roman Abramovich (2003/04 and 2004/05).
City's Climb: Manchester City were ninth in 2007/08; after the 2008 Abu Dhabi takeover they reached the top by 2015/16.
The Record Season: Chelsea's €562m net spend in 2022/23 is the most by any club in a single Premier League season.
2026 Finish: City's €189m net summer moved them back above United, €2.16bn to €2.11bn.

Data includes: Manchester City, Manchester United, Chelsea, Arsenal, Tottenham Hotspur, Liverpool, Newcastle United, West Ham United, Aston Villa, Fulham, and other historical top-ten contenders.

Data Source: Transfermarkt, Premier League transfer income and expenditures by season (1992/93–2026/27)

## How it works
- Net spend = transfer fees paid minus fees received, as recorded by Transfermarkt, in euros. Loan fees are included, as on Transfermarkt's default table.
- Only Premier League seasons count. A club's spending in seasons outside the top flight is not included (e.g. City 1996–2000 and 2001/02, Newcastle 2009/10 and 2016/17, Villa 2016–19, Leeds 2004–2020).
- Each step is one season (summer and January windows). The last step, 2026/27, is the 2026 summer window only.
- Bars use club colours and crests. Birmingham City and Stoke City (10th in 2010/11–2011/12 and 2012/13–2013/14) have no crest in our logo folder, so they show colour only; send me their crests and I'll re-render.
