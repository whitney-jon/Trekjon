# Who has earned the most in MLB history?

Style: **Baseball Notebook** (Notebook hand-drawn skin with a blue baseball header and red rule), rendered with `pl_notebook_style.py` and `NB_PRESET=baseball`.

## Description
In 2002, Barry Bonds was the highest-paid player in baseball history, with just under $100 million in career earnings. Today the record is almost five times that. Who has earned the most in MLB history?

This race tracks career on-field earnings (salary plus signing bonuses and deferred money, as paid) from 2002 to 2026, with each bar in the colours of the team paying the player that season. Bonds leads into the late 2000s, then Alex Rodriguez, on the record $252 million contract he signed with Texas and the deal he later signed with the Yankees, passes him in 2008 and never looks back, finishing on $485 million. Behind him, Derek Jeter, Albert Pujols and Miguel Cabrera take turns at No. 2, before Justin Verlander climbs to second in 2024 and Max Scherzer and Mike Trout close in.

For European fans: Venezuela's Miguel Cabrera ($393m) is third all-time, and the Dominican Republic's Albert Pujols ($342m) is fifth.

Key Highlights:
A-Rod's Record: Alex Rodriguez passed Barry Bonds in 2008 and finished on $485 million.
The Chasers: Jeter, Pujols and Cabrera were all No. 2 at some point.
Verlander's Climb: Justin Verlander passed Cabrera for second in 2024 and is on $411 million.
Still Active: Max Scherzer ($365m) and Mike Trout ($339m) are still climbing.

Data includes: Alex Rodriguez, Justin Verlander, Miguel Cabrera, Max Scherzer, Albert Pujols, Zack Greinke, Mike Trout, Clayton Kershaw, Derek Jeter, Barry Bonds, and other historical top-ten contenders.

Data Source: Spotrac, MLB player career earnings (cash by season, through 2026); Baseball Data Hub / Lahman salary database (Chipper Jones, Cal Ripken Jr., David Cone, Ivan Rodriguez, Bernie Williams)

## How it works
- Values are career cash earnings added up season by season: base salary, signing bonuses and deferred money in the year it was paid (Spotrac's "cash" figure). Endorsements are not included.
- This is why the numbers are higher than salary-only lists such as Baseball-Reference's: A-Rod is $485m here, against about $399m in base salary.
- Deferred money counts when paid: Bobby Bonilla's Mets payments and Ken Griffey Jr.'s Reds payments keep adding after they retired, and Shohei Ohtani's $700m deal shows only $2m a year because $680m is deferred until 2034–2043. Juan Soto's $75m signing bonus counts in 2025.
- Team colours follow the team that paid the player each season (the active team, not a team still paying retained or deferred money). Retired players keep their last team's colours.
- Five players have no season-by-season cash on Spotrac (Chipper Jones, Cal Ripken Jr., David Cone, Ivan Rodriguez, Bernie Williams), so their figures are salaries from the Lahman database via Baseball Data Hub. Of these, only Chipper Jones reaches the top 10 (2011–2013).
- Players whose earnings start before 2002 carry their earlier career earnings into the first frame (e.g. Bonds $99m, Maddux $93m at the end of 2002).
- 2026 is the full 2026 season.
