# Who has earned the most in NHL history?

Style: **Hockey Notebook** (Notebook hand-drawn skin with a black ice-hockey header, ice-blue underline), rendered with `pl_notebook_style.py` and `NB_PRESET=hockey`. Team logos replace flags and change as players move teams.

## Description
In 1997, Mario Lemieux was the highest-paid player in NHL history, with $38 million in career earnings. Today the record is $170 million. Who has earned the most in NHL history?

This race tracks NHL career earnings season by season from 1996-97 to 2025-26, with each bar in the colours and logo of the player's team that season. Lemieux and Wayne Gretzky lead the late 1990s, Mark Messier takes over in 2001, and Jaromír Jágr becomes the NHL's top earner in 2003. Chris Pronger briefly passes him in 2014, but Jágr takes it back two years later. Sidney Crosby finally overtakes Jágr in 2019, and in 2025 Alex Ovechkin passes Crosby to become the highest-paid player in NHL history, on $170.7 million.

For European fans: Russia's Ovechkin ($170.7m) and Evgeni Malkin ($142.5m), Slovenia's Anže Kopitar ($141.4m), Sweden's Erik Karlsson ($130.6m) and Czechia's Jágr ($119m) are all among the biggest earners in NHL history.

Key Highlights:
Jágr's Reign: Jaromír Jágr was the NHL's top career earner for most of 2003–2018.
Crosby's Turn: Sidney Crosby passed Jágr in 2019 and led for six seasons.
Ovechkin's Record: Alex Ovechkin passed Crosby in 2025 and leads on $170.7 million.
The Russian 1-3: Ovechkin and Malkin are two of the top three.

Data includes: Alex Ovechkin, Sidney Crosby, Evgeni Malkin, Anže Kopitar, Drew Doughty, Erik Karlsson, Patrick Kane, Carey Price, Shea Weber, Jaromír Jágr, and other historical top-ten contenders.

Data Source: HockeyZonePlus salary histories (NHL earnings by season, 1980s–2025-26); Spotrac (teams by season, Toews 2025-26)

## How it works
- Values are NHL earnings (salary, signing bonuses and NHL buyout payments) added up season by season, as recorded by HockeyZonePlus. Endorsements are not included.
- NHL money only: Jágr's three KHL seasons with Omsk (2008–11, $21m), Kovalchuk's KHL seasons, Fedorov's KHL years, 2004-05 lockout pay in Russia, junior/minor-league pay and post-playing salaries as coaches or executives (Roy, Gretzky, Shanahan) are removed. That is why Jágr shows $119m, not the $140m HockeyZonePlus lists as his total hockey earnings.
- Years are season-end years (2026 = 2025-26). The cancelled 2004-05 season has no step. The 2026-27 season in progress is not included.
- Earnings from before 1996-97 are carried into the first frame (e.g. Lemieux $38.3m, Gretzky $35.7m).
- Bars and logos follow the player's team at the end of each season. Players who are retired or no longer playing (including those paid through buyouts or long-term injury) keep the last team they played for (e.g. Pronger Flyers, Lecavalier Kings, Weber Canadiens).
- Kariya (1995–2003) and Fedorov (2004) use the Mighty Ducks logo; Pronger's Anaheim years use the Ducks logo.
- Jonathan Toews' 2025-26 Winnipeg salary ($2m) comes from Spotrac, as his HockeyZonePlus page stops at 2022-23.
