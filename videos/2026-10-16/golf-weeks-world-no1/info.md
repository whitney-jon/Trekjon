# Who has spent the most weeks as golf's world No. 1?

## Description
When the Official World Golf Ranking began in April 1986, Bernhard Langer was the first world No. 1, and Seve Ballesteros took over three weeks later. Forty years on, who has spent the most weeks at the top of world golf?

This race tracks total weeks at world No. 1 from 1986 to the 26 October 2026 ranking. Greg Norman ruled the late 1980s and 1990s with 331 weeks, Tiger Woods passed him in August 2004 and finished on a record 683, and Scottie Scheffler's unbroken run since May 2023 has carried him to third place all-time.

For European fans: Nick Faldo (97 weeks), Rory McIlroy (122), Seve Ballesteros (61), Luke Donald (56), Jon Rahm (52) and Ian Woosnam (50) all reach the top ten.

Key Highlights:
The Shark's Era: Greg Norman spent 331 weeks at No. 1 between 1986 and 1998.
Tiger's Record: Tiger Woods passed Norman in August 2004 and held No. 1 for 281 straight weeks from 2005 to 2010.
Rory's Spells: McIlroy has had nine spells at No. 1 since 2012, totalling 122 weeks.
Scheffler's Streak: Scottie Scheffler has been No. 1 every week since 21 May 2023, taking him to 215 weeks.

Data includes: Tiger Woods, Greg Norman, Scottie Scheffler, Dustin Johnson, Rory McIlroy, Nick Faldo, Seve Ballesteros, Luke Donald, Jon Rahm, Jason Day, and other historical top-ten contenders.

Data Source: Wikipedia, List of world number one male golfers (spells 1986–2026, as of 27 Sep 2026); Sky Sports (Scheffler's 178th consecutive week, 12 Oct 2026)
