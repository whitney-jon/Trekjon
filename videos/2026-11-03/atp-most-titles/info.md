## Description
At the end of 2008, Roger Federer had 57 titles, tenth on the all-time list. Jimmy Connors' 109 looked untouchable. Who has won the most ATP titles?

This race tracks every man's career singles titles (Open Era, ATP tour-level) from 2008 to today. Federer climbs past Agassi, Sampras, Borg, Laver and McEnroe, then passes Lendl for No. 2 in 2017 and finishes on 103. Rafael Nadal climbs to 92. Novak Djokovic, 11 titles in 2008, wins his 100th in Geneva in 2025 and his 102nd in Beijing this October, one behind Federer and seven behind Connors.

Key Highlights:
Connors' Record: 109 titles, the Open Era record since he retired, and No. 1 in every frame.
Federer's Climb: from 10th to 2nd, passing Ivan Lendl's 94 in 2017.
Djokovic Closing In: 102 titles, one behind Federer, with the Paris Masters and ATP Finals still to play in 2026.
The Big Three: Federer, Djokovic and Nadal are now three of the top five.

Data includes: Jimmy Connors, Roger Federer, Novak Djokovic, Ivan Lendl, Rafael Nadal, John McEnroe, Rod Laver, Björn Borg, Ilie Năstase, Pete Sampras, Guillermo Vilas, Andre Agassi.

Data Source: Wikipedia career statistics pages (Federer, Djokovic, Nadal titles by year) and the list of Open Era title leaders; totals checked against Too Many Rackets (16 Oct 2026), Tennis My Life stats and the ATP Tour (Beijing 2026).

## How it works
- Open Era (since 1968) men's singles tour-level titles, including the Olympics and the season-ending finals. Totals before 2008 are carried into the first frame (Federer 53, Nadal 23, Djokovic 7).
- Retired players' totals are fixed: Connors 109, Lendl 94, McEnroe 77, Laver 72, Borg 66, Năstase 64, Sampras 64, Vilas 62, Agassi 60.
- 2026* is the season so far, up to Djokovic's Beijing title in October. Paris and the ATP Finals are still to come. If Djokovic wins either, re-render with 103 (a tie with Federer).
- Ivan Lendl's flag is Czechoslovakia, the nation he represented for almost all of his career (he became a US citizen in 1992).
- Rod Laver's 72 and Borg's 66 follow the Open Era title lists, which three sources agree on.
