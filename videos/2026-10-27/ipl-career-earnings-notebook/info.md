# Who has earned the most in IPL history?

Style: **IPL Notebook** (Notebook hand-drawn skin with an IPL navy header and orange underline), rendered with `pl_notebook_style.py`, `NB_PRESET=ipl` and the new `--theme cricket` (cricket-ball icon). Country flags on the bars.

## Description
In 2008, MS Dhoni was the first big signing of the Indian Premier League, on $1.5 million a season. 19 seasons later, three Indian icons have each earned more than $30 million. Who has earned the most in IPL history?

This race tracks IPL career salary earnings in US dollars, season by season, from the first IPL in 2008 to IPL 2026. Dhoni leads from day one and stays No. 1 for 17 seasons, with Gautam Gambhir and Yuvraj Singh chasing him in the early years, until Rohit Sharma finally passes him in 2025 and finishes top on $34.2 million, ahead of Virat Kohli and Dhoni.

For European fans: England's Kevin Pietersen and Andrew Flintoff were the IPL's first $1.55 million overseas buys in 2009, and Australia's Andrew Symonds was second only to Dhoni after the first season.

Key Highlights:
Dhoni's Reign: MS Dhoni was the IPL's top career earner from 2008 to 2024.
Yuvraj's Record Deal: Yuvraj Singh's $2.5 million (₹16 crore) in 2015 took him within half a million of Dhoni.
Rohit's Record: Rohit Sharma passed Dhoni in 2025 and leads on $34.2 million.
The $30m Club: Rohit, Kohli and Dhoni are the only players past $30 million.

Data includes: Rohit Sharma, Virat Kohli, MS Dhoni, Ravindra Jadeja, Sunil Narine, Suresh Raina, Rishabh Pant, Gautam Gambhir, AB de Villiers, Sanju Samson, and other historical top-ten contenders.

Data Source: iplsalary.in player salary histories (2008–2026); SalarySport IPL player pages (players missing from iplsalary.in); US Federal Reserve rupee exchange rates (FRED)

## How it works
- Values are IPL salaries (auction price or retention fee per season) in US dollars, added up season by season, 2008 to 2026. Endorsements and prize money are not included.
- Dollars: 2008–2013 contracts were set in dollars and are shown at their dollar value. From 2014 salaries are in rupees and are converted at the average rate during that IPL season (April–May; Sep–Nov for 2020, played in the UAE), from FRED: e.g. ₹59.8 in 2014, ₹76.7 in 2022, ₹85.4 in 2025, ₹94.5 in 2026.
- In rupees Kohli finishes top (₹232.3cr v Rohit ₹230.7cr), but the rupee's fall in 2026 makes his ₹21 crore worth less in dollars, so Rohit leads in dollar terms.
- 2008–2013 IPL contracts were set in US dollars. The sources convert overseas players at each season's exchange rate but some Indian players at a flat ₹40 or ₹46 per dollar; to compare everyone fairly, every dollar contract is converted at the same season rates (₹40.2 in 2008, ₹49.1 in 2009, ₹46.3 in 2010, ₹46.0 in 2011, ₹50.3 in 2012, ₹53.3 in 2013). This is why some totals are ₹2–5 crore above the sources' figures (e.g. Kohli ₹232.3cr v ₹230.2cr, Dhoni ₹201.3cr v ₹196.84cr).
- iplsalary.in is missing several players, so their histories come from SalarySport: Sachin Tendulkar, KL Rahul, Kieron Pollard, Shane Watson, Dwayne Bravo, Ben Stokes, Andrew Symonds, Sourav Ganguly, Rahul Dravid and others. SalarySport has no 2022–23 entries, so these were added from the published auction/retention prices: KL Rahul ₹17cr (2022, 2023), Pollard ₹6cr (2022), Bravo ₹4.4cr (2022), Ambati Rayudu ₹6.75cr (2022, 2023); Stokes' ₹16.25cr is counted in 2023 (he withdrew from 2024).
- Bars show the player's country flag (Sunil Narine, Kieron Pollard and Dwayne Bravo: Trinidad & Tobago; Andre Russell and Chris Gayle: Jamaica).
