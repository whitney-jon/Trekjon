# Who has earned the most in IPL history?

Style: **IPL Notebook** (Notebook hand-drawn skin with an IPL navy header and orange underline), rendered with `pl_notebook_style.py`, `NB_PRESET=ipl` and the new `--theme cricket` (cricket-ball icon). Country flags on the bars.

## Description
In 2008, MS Dhoni was the first big signing of the Indian Premier League, earning ₹6 crore a season. 19 seasons later, three Indian icons have each earned more than ₹200 crore. Who has earned the most in IPL history?

This race tracks IPL career salary earnings season by season from the first IPL in 2008 to IPL 2026. Dhoni leads from day one and stays No. 1 for 15 seasons, with Gautam Gambhir and Yuvraj Singh chasing him in the early years. Then Rohit Sharma passes Dhoni in 2023, and in 2026 Virat Kohli, a one-club man at RCB, takes the record for the first time, finishing on ₹232.3 crore.

For European fans: England's Kevin Pietersen ($1.55m in 2009) and Andrew Flintoff were the IPL's first big overseas buys, and Australia's Andrew Symonds was second only to Dhoni after the first season.

Key Highlights:
Dhoni's Reign: MS Dhoni was the IPL's top career earner from 2008 to 2022.
Rohit's Turn: Rohit Sharma passed Dhoni in 2023 and led for three seasons.
Kohli's Record: Virat Kohli's ₹21 crore salaries in 2025 and 2026 took him top, on ₹232.3 crore.
The ₹200 crore club: Kohli, Rohit and Dhoni are the only players past ₹200 crore.

Data includes: Virat Kohli, Rohit Sharma, MS Dhoni, Ravindra Jadeja, Rishabh Pant, Sunil Narine, KL Rahul, Sanju Samson, Shreyas Iyer, Hardik Pandya, and other historical top-ten contenders.

Data Source: iplsalary.in player salary histories (2008–2026); SalarySport IPL player pages (players missing from iplsalary.in)

## How it works
- Values are IPL salaries (auction price or retention fee per season) in ₹ crore, added up season by season, 2008 to 2026. Endorsements and prize money are not included.
- 2008–2013 IPL contracts were set in US dollars. The sources convert overseas players at each season's exchange rate but some Indian players at a flat ₹40 or ₹46 per dollar; to compare everyone fairly, every dollar contract is converted at the same season rates (₹40.2 in 2008, ₹49.1 in 2009, ₹46.3 in 2010, ₹46.0 in 2011, ₹50.3 in 2012, ₹53.3 in 2013). This is why some totals are ₹2–5 crore above the sources' figures (e.g. Kohli ₹232.3cr v ₹230.2cr, Dhoni ₹201.3cr v ₹196.84cr).
- iplsalary.in is missing several players, so their histories come from SalarySport: Sachin Tendulkar, KL Rahul, Kieron Pollard, Shane Watson, Dwayne Bravo, Ben Stokes, Andrew Symonds, Sourav Ganguly, Rahul Dravid and others. SalarySport has no 2022–23 entries, so these were added from the published auction/retention prices: KL Rahul ₹17cr (2022, 2023), Pollard ₹6cr (2022), Bravo ₹4.4cr (2022), Ambati Rayudu ₹6.75cr (2022, 2023); Stokes' ₹16.25cr is counted in 2023 (he withdrew from 2024).
- Bars show the player's country flag (Sunil Narine, Kieron Pollard and Dwayne Bravo: Trinidad & Tobago; Andre Russell and Chris Gayle: Jamaica).
