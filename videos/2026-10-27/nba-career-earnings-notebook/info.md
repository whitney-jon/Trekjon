# Who has earned the most in NBA history?

Style: **NBA Notebook** (Notebook hand-drawn skin with an NBA blue header and red underline), rendered with `pl_notebook_style.py` and `NB_PRESET=nba` (`--theme basketball`). Team logos replace flags and change as players move teams.

## Description
In 1997, Michael Jordan's back-to-back $30 million seasons made him the highest-paid player in NBA history, on $58.9 million. Today the record is almost ten times that. Who has earned the most in NBA history?

This race tracks NBA career salary earnings season by season from 1996-97 to 2025-26, with each bar in the colours and logo of the player's team. Jordan leads until Patrick Ewing passes him in 1999, Shaquille O'Neal takes over in 2003 and holds it for a decade, and Kevin Garnett becomes No. 1 in 2013. Then LeBron James passes Garnett in 2021 and pulls away, finishing on $584 million, ahead of Kevin Durant and Stephen Curry.

For European fans: Germany's Dirk Nowitzki ($255.4m), who spent all 21 seasons with Dallas, was fourth on the all-time list in 2017, behind only Garnett, Kobe and Shaq.

Key Highlights:
Jordan's Peak: Michael Jordan earned $63.3 million in 1996-97 and 1997-98, the biggest single-season salaries of the era.
Shaq's Decade: Shaquille O'Neal was the NBA's top career earner from 2003 to 2012.
The Big Ticket: Kevin Garnett led from 2013 to 2020, finishing on $343.9 million.
King James: LeBron passed Garnett in 2021 and leads on $584 million.

Data includes: LeBron James, Kevin Durant, Stephen Curry, James Harden, Paul George, Chris Paul, Kawhi Leonard, Jimmy Butler, Damian Lillard, Kevin Garnett, and other historical top-ten contenders.

Data Source: Basketball-Reference player salary histories (1979-80 to 2025-26); Spotrac season salary rankings (to find contenders)

## How it works
- Values are NBA salaries as listed by Basketball-Reference, added up season by season. Endorsements are not included.
- Years are season-end years (2026 = 2025-26). The 2026-27 season in progress is not included (e.g. LeBron $584.0m here).
- Earnings before 1996-97 are carried into the first frame (e.g. Jordan $58.9m, Ewing $53.2m).
- Bars and logos follow the team that paid the player most that season; retired players keep their last team. Carmelo Anthony's 2018-19 season is shown as Houston, where he played (Atlanta/Chicago paid him after a trade and release).
- Why not Spotrac: its NBA earnings pages are incomplete (e.g. LeBron's stop at 2021), so Basketball-Reference is used.
- Contenders were found from Spotrac's season salary rankings 1990–2025, plus 50 hand-picked players; 146 players' full salary histories were checked.
- The Spurs bar uses their black (not silver) so the logo stays visible.
